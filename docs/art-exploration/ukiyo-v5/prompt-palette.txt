A terrain palette study for a Civilization-like strategy game. Use only the ukiyo-e woodblock TECHNIQUE of the references: fine black key-block outlines, flat colour areas, soft bokashi gradients, patterned foliage and waves, cream paper texture. The SUBJECTS are not Japanese: people, buildings and ships belong to the civilization shown (Greek, Egyptian, Roman, Persian, medieval European and so on). Camera straight down on the map, like the reference game screen. No grid lines.
One single continuous top-down map filling the image, with every terrain type flowing into its neighbours with soft natural borders. Each terrain has its own clearly different colour AND brightness, so they never blend together: deep sea dark indigo #1F3A5F with Hokusai waves; shallow coast bright turquoise #4F9BA8; grassland fresh light green #8DB255; plains warm golden ochre #D9B45A; desert pale sand #EAD9A6 with dune lines; forest very dark blue-green #2E5A43 dense canopy; jungle saturated emerald #2F8A4E with palms; hills olive-brown #A08A4E with contour lines; mountains slate grey-blue #6E7C8C with white snow caps; tundra grey-lilac #A9A39A; arctic white #F2F2EE with pale blue shadows; swamp dark teal-olive #4F6B55 with reed hatching and dark pools; rivers bright blue #3F7FBF with a thin light edge. Terrain colours are muted to medium saturation; vermilion, gold and strong player colours are NOT used on terrain. Each terrain has a small black caption on a cream label. Along the bottom edge, a strip of 13 labelled colour swatches showing the terrain colours. No units, no cities.
