One complete CITY SCREEN for a strategy game, landscape 3:2, filling the image edge to edge, designed as a single cohesive work in the style of a Momoyama-period Japanese folding screen (byobu): gold-leaf ground, raised gold clouds (suyari-gasumi), thick mineral pigments (malachite green, azurite blue, shell white, earth ochre, a little cinnabar), black lacquer frames with small gilt-bronze corner fittings, fine confident ink outlines. It must look hand-made and beautiful, with calm areas of plain gold, not busy everywhere.
The first reference image is the city painting to use: keep that exact isometric Babylon, its walls, gate, ziggurat, palace, barracks yard, library, granary, river and sea, in parallel projection.
The second reference image shows the classic 1991 Civilization city screen arrangement, which this screen pays homage to: keep that ARRANGEMENT of information, but redraw every part in the byobu style.
LAYOUT:
TOP: a narrow black-lacquer title bar with gold lettering "BABYLON" in the centre with a small round size seal "8", "1250 BC" at the right. Just below it, across the full width, a row of 8 small painted citizens standing on a strip of gold: 3 happy with raised arms, 3 content, 1 unhappy with bowed head, and, set apart, an entertainer with a lute.
CENTRE, the largest area: the city painting from the first reference, emerging from gold clouds that frame it and soften its edges; the mouse is over the Granary, which has a thin white outline and a small paper label "Granary".
LEFT COLUMN, two panels, each a small black-lacquer-framed panel on gold-leaf ground with a title written in sumi on a narrow white-paper strip: "City Resources" with rows of small painted icons, food as 12 grain sheaves (4 drawn pale as surplus), shields as 6 round shields, trade as 8 square-holed coins, and under them 2 vases, 3 gold coins and 3 oil lamps; below it "City Map", a tiny top-down painted landscape with the 21 squares around the city in a rounded diamond, 8 worked squares marked with tiny sheaf, shield and coin icons.
RIGHT COLUMN, one tall panel "Improvements": six small painted buildings with their names in sumi, Palace, Granary, Temple, Barracks, City Walls, Library.
BOTTOM ROW, three panels of the same kind: "Units Present" with a spearman behind a palisade and an archer, and under a fine line "Supported" with a spearman, a chariot and a settler; "Food Storage" as a woven basket two thirds full of grain sheaves; "Production" with a small painting of a marketplace under timber scaffolding, the word "Marketplace", a bar of 20 small round shields with 14 filled, and three small lacquer buttons with gold letters "CHANGE", "BUY", "AUTO".
At the bottom right corner two lacquer buttons "RENAME" and "EXIT".
All panels share one frame design, one icon size and one lettering style, a clean readable serif in sumi on paper strips or gold on lacquer, spelled exactly as given. No other text. The gold clouds tie the panels and the city together into one screen.
