A concept sheet for a Civilization-like strategy game in exactly the same ukiyo-e woodblock style as the reference game screens: flat colour inside fine black key-block lines, indigo and Prussian blue, sage and ochre ground, vermilion accents, cream paper, Hokusai waves. The camera looks straight down, as on the reference map. Thin cream gutters separate the panels, and each panel has a small plain black caption on the cream gutter below it.
The sheet shows CITIES as a grid of 12 square map panels, 4 rows by 3 columns, each panel a piece of continuous painted grassland with a little forest and a river.
Rows, top to bottom: Ancient, Medieval, Industrial, Modern. Columns, left to right: Town (size 2), City (size 6), Metropolis (size 14).
Every city is painted into the ground with no plinth, base plate, outline or drop shadow: a dense core of roofs and walls that thins into houses, lanes, gardens and small fields fading into the grass around it, like the walled towns in the reference. Bigger cities spread further, but a city never covers more than about one and a half map squares. A small blue square badge with the size number sits at the top-left of each city. Ancient and medieval cities can be walled; industrial ones add brick, chimneys and a railway; modern ones add towers and highways.
